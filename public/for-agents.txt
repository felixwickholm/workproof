1. THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH
====================================================================

WorkProof is for City of Espoo talent events. Companies see real work and score it; the
AI never scores. A professional does a short real task from an Espoo company. A named
person at the company scores the work from 1 to 5 on each of the task's skills, writes a
reason, and chooses Meet or Not now. As a safeguard, the name stays hidden until the
professional accepts. Tested: 0 of 79 planted identity details reached a reviewer.

(This paragraph is SUMMARY in src/app/for-judges/facts.ts, word for word the same as the
README lead and /llms.txt.)

The core idea in one line: replace the AI-written CV with a piece of real work, read blind
and scored by a named human, and give the city a measurable path from challenge to hire.

2. THE PROBLEM THE CITY OF ESPOO POSED, AND WHY IT MATTERS
====================================================================

2.1 The challenge question, in the City of Espoo's words

  "How can recruitment and matchmaking events organized by City of Espoo help companies
  and talent relevantly find and connect with each other, despite AI-generated job search
  materials and AI-assisted recruitment processes are becoming common?"

  "...solutions that help create stronger signal and less noise throughout the process of
  registering and attending an event..."

  "...automated profiling or automated decision-making about individuals without human
  involvement should not be a part of the solution."

2.2 The specific problems the brief lists

  P1. Companies are hard to engage. They do not see the city as a source of candidates,
      and assume the city's pool holds no suitable people. A free service is doubted.
  P2. Companies struggle to describe their talent needs clearly, and too late (just
      before the event day).
  P3. Organisers receive many applications but cannot see real capability, especially
      for technology skills.
  P4. Companies are overwhelmed by large volumes of irrelevant approaches at events.
  P5. Professionals struggle to describe their competencies and transferable skills.
  P6. CVs and messages are written with AI and analysed with AI, so matching risks
      resting on synthetic representations instead of genuine capability.
  P7. Event platforms with matching algorithms feel cumbersome, and may crash when
      everyone logs in at the start of an event.
  P8. International recruitment adds language, permit, relocation and onboarding
      questions.
  P9. Solutions must not reinforce bias or disadvantage people by nationality, language,
      educational background or career path.

2.3 The brief's rules for solutions

  R1. No automated profiling or automated decision-making about individuals without
      human involvement.
  R2. Use synthetic data.
  R3. Minimise personal data processing.
  R4. Show how privacy would be protected in a real deployment in compliance with the
      EU AI Act.

2.4 Judging criteria

  Impact & Relevance                    45%
  Innovation & User Experience          35%
  Responsible & Feasible Execution      20%

2.5 Why this matters to Espoo

  The city runs free recruitment and matchmaking events, online, in person and hybrid.
  Its value to companies depends on one thing: whether the people it brings are worth
  meeting. When every CV reads well because AI wrote it, a CV no longer separates
  candidates, and a company has no reason to trust a free public pipeline. A piece of
  real work on the company's own task is a signal AI-written documents cannot fake in the
  same way, and it gives the city something it can count: work done, work scored, people
  invited, talks held, hires. This turns the city's events from a sign-up list into a
  measurable pipeline, which is the evidence the city needs to win companies back (P1).

3. WHAT WORKPROOF IS: THE LOOP AND THE THREE VIEWS
====================================================================

3.1 The loop

  task -> work -> blind proof -> a named person scores it, writes feedback and chooses
  Meet or Not now -> the person accepts -> only then is the name shown -> both sides
  confirm they met -> the city counts each step.

  Step by step:
  1. A company pastes a job-ad link, writes one sentence, or picks a role. An assistant
     asks at most two short questions (what the work is, and how big), then drafts a
     challenge: title, task, material, what the person hands in, 3 to 5 core skills, and
     what to look for per skill. The company edits any line and approves it. Wishes about
     the person (age, native language, school) are set aside with a reason.
     (src/lib/service/assist.ts, src/app/api/challenges/assist, /api/challenges/from-ad,
     /api/challenges/draft, POST /api/challenges)
  2. A professional picks the challenge (formats: Choose, Make, Fix, Read), does it, and
     writes how they think while working. Two follow-up questions ask about their own
     choices, quoting their own words. Before Send, they see exactly what the company
     will read. (/challenges/[id]/work, POST /api/followups, POST /api/preview)
  3. The server stores the original and builds a blind proof: known names, contacts,
     schools, places, employers and personal details are replaced by one neutral token,
     "[hidden]". (src/lib/blind)
  4. A reviewer at the company reads blind proofs in arrival order, gives 1 to 5 points
     for each core skill, writes a reason, and chooses Invite (Meet), Not now, or Score
     only, decide later. The total shows out of 10 (average points x 2).
     (/company/review, POST /api/decisions, src/lib/domain/scoring.ts)
  5. The person reads the scores and the reason, and accepts or declines the invitation.
     Only after accepting does that company see the name and contact.
     (/my-work, POST /api/meetings/[id]/accept, src/lib/service/meetings.ts)
  6. Both sides confirm the talk took place. The company can record a follow-up and a
     hire. The city sees every step as counts. (/city, GET /api/city)

3.2 The three views (one app; switch at the top left)

  Job seeker  (route /)
    Pick a challenge from an Espoo company, do it in 8 to 30 minutes and write how they
    think, see what the company will read, answer two follow-up questions, then accept or
    decline an invitation. A declined company never sees the name. "My work" (/my-work)
    shows the person's own work, what was hidden, the reviewer's scores and reason, and a
    portfolio link to share.

  Company  (route /employer)
    Paste a job-ad link or say the need in one sentence, approve a challenge with 3 to 5
    core skills and a number of places, read the work without names in arrival order,
    score each core skill from 1 to 5 with a written reason, and invite people to meet.
    Each task has "top work": scored proofs sorted by the reviewers' own totals, still
    without names. (/company, /company/review, /employer/challenges/[id])

  City of Espoo  (route /city)
    See outcomes, not sign-ups: challenges taken, scored, invited, said yes, talked,
    follow-ups agreed and hires. Per company: work received, answered and still owed,
    and median reply time. Make a challenge for a company from its job ad, draft a
    reminder to a company with work waiting (nothing is sent), and export the list of
    talks as CSV. A map of Espoo shows where the work goes. An audit log (/city/log).
    Slots and labels, never names.

3.3 Supporting surfaces

  /try       A 2-minute task on a phone: "What's your dream job?", a short task, then
             "This is what a company sees."
  /table     A big-screen mode for a booth: a QR code and a 6-character code. A phone
             that scans it joins the same demo event. The host invites a proof with a
             two-sentence reason; the visitor accepts or declines on the phone; only
             after a yes does the first name appear on the big screen. The big screen
             shows labels and tasks, never free text.
  /?tour=1   A 17-card guided walkthrough over the real app, in four chapters (Job
             seeker, Company, City of Espoo, the goal). It starts by itself on the first
             visit. Each view's home has a "Show me" button that starts its chapter.
  /how-it-works  The product in six plain steps.
  /for-judges    The evidence page: criteria, evals, architecture, data and privacy.
  /brief, /brief.pdf  "WorkProof: the brief and the build", a 6-page explainer.

3.4 Second look (one proof, more chances)

  If the person turns it on, a proof that got Not now, or no answer within 48 hours, can
  be read by another company whose task shares at least 2 core skills. It compares tasks,
  never people. It is off by default, only the owner can switch it, and it can be
  withdrawn. A company that shares never shares its scores.
  (src/lib/matching/second-look.ts, src/lib/service/sharing.ts)

5.2 The brief's rules

R1  No automated profiling or automated decision-making about individuals.
    -> WorkProof has no automated decision feature. A decision is recorded only with the
       name of a reviewer on the company's registered list, the reviewer's own points
       for each core skill, and a written reason of at least 4 words and 20 characters.
       A name that reads as software ("AI bot") is refused. The guard rejects scores,
       ranks and verdicts in any AI output. Every decision is an audit entry with actor
       "human"; the city counts automated decisions over stored decisions and the log.
    -> /city/log, /how-it-works
    -> src/lib/service/decisions.ts, src/lib/service/reviewers.ts,
       src/lib/domain/decision-rules.ts, src/lib/domain/scoring.ts,
       src/lib/guard/no-judgement.ts, src/lib/ai/guarded.ts,
       src/lib/service/views/automated.ts
    -> Eval 3: 70 of 70 planted judging phrases rejected; 33 of 33 neutral phrases
       passed; 575 of 575 text fields from 32 designer outputs pass; decisions without a
       name or feedback refused with HTTP 400; 48 stored decisions all named; HTTP 400
       for "AI bot", HTTP 403 for an unregistered name; scores refused without points,
       with a skill missing, or from a software name; 0 automated decisions and 0
       decision audit lines by a non-human.

R2  Synthetic data.
    -> Every person, company, task, proof and decision is invented. Emails use
       example.org; phone numbers are placeholders. The pictures and clips in
       public/demo were made for this project.
    -> README "Data"; src/lib/seed

R3  Minimise personal data.
    -> No account and no profile. The person's private key stays in their browser and
       travels in a header (x-workproof-key) or a request body, never in an address. An
       event proof needs a name and one contact only, so the company can reach the
       person after they accept. A practice task asks for a first name only, and it can
       be skipped. Photos are re-encoded in the browser without metadata; the server
       refuses a JPEG that still has EXIF or XMP. Answers are stored as text. The city
       sees counts and labels, never names.
    -> /try, /my-work, /city
    -> src/lib/client/local.ts, src/lib/http/talent-key.ts,
       src/components/seeker/picture.ts, src/lib/http/jpeg.ts
    -> Eval 4: 0 of 47 talent ids in company and city responses; 0 of 36 pictures of
       people who have not said yes; key in a header only, absent from the audit log;
       practice proof keeps no name; 5 bad pictures refused (EXIF, /demo path, PNG,
       video, 1 byte over 300 kB), clean JPEGs accepted; city responses, leaderboards,
       share matches and the meeting export carry 0 names, contacts or keys over 4 story
       stages.

R4  Privacy under the EU AI Act.
    -> See section 9: the AI is kept to a narrow preparatory role (drafting tasks and
       questions), human oversight, logging, AI labels, no voice or face analysis. The
       full position is in docs/responsible-ai.md.

6. WHAT THE CITY OF ESPOO ASKED FOR AT THE BOOTH, AND WHAT WAS BUILT
====================================================================

The team spoke with City of Espoo staff at the booth on 13 November and showed a live
demo on 14 November. Their questions shaped the product. Our notes, paraphrased:

  Asked: Where do the tasks come from? The city cannot write them, and companies must
         spend almost no effort.
  Built: A company pastes a job-ad link or writes one sentence; the assistant drafts the
         challenge. The city can make it for the company from a public job ad and invite
         the company to see the work already done.
         (/company, /city, src/lib/service/assist.ts, src/lib/http/ad-fetch.ts)

  Asked: Make performance visible: how well people match what the company expects, as a
         score (for example 0 to 10) and a per-task leaderboard.
  Built: A named reviewer scores 1 to 5 per core skill; the total shows out of 10; each
         task has "top work" sorted by the reviewers' own totals, without names.
         Software never gives or suggests a number.
         (src/lib/domain/scoring.ts, src/lib/service/views/leaderboard.ts,
         GET /api/companies/[id]/tasks/[taskId]/top)

  Asked: Impact numbers: how many took a challenge, how many proofs each company
         reviewed, how many meetings came out of it.
  Built: The city view "From challenge to hire" and per-company "Scored or waiting".
         (/city, src/lib/service/views/city.ts)

  Asked: Let a company that hires one person point others to the rest.
  Built: Second look: with the person's consent, unanswered or Not-now work reaches a
         company whose task shares at least 2 core skills. Scores are never sent along.
         (src/lib/matching/second-look.ts; Eval 4 "top proofs and shared work stay
         blind": scores sent along: false)

  Asked: A mix of technical, commercial and design roles.
  Built: 40 challenges across tech, business and design work (section 4).

  Asked: The event is the first touch; the final assessment happens face to face.
  Built: Work is scored before the event; an invitation ends in a talk at the event,
         which both sides confirm; the meeting starts from the person's own work.

Earlier, on 13 November, staff named three risks. Our answers: a company hires only a
few (Second look); friction must stay low (no login, no account, demo challenges take 8
to 30 minutes, the stated length is capped at 30); AI can do the task (AI is allowed,
two follow-up questions ask about the person's own choices, and the meeting starts from
the work). Source: "Shaped at the Espoo booth" in facts.ts, README and /llms-full.txt.

7. ARCHITECTURE
====================================================================

7.1 Stack

  Next.js 16 (App Router, cacheComponents on, standalone output), React 19, TypeScript
  strict, zod at the API edge, Tailwind, Vitest. Node.js 22. No database, no login, no
  keys needed to run. About 43,000 lines of TypeScript outside tests in src; 150 test
  and eval files. Over 100 commits, first commit 2026-11-14 02:25 EET. Nothing was reused from
  earlier projects (README).

7.2 Layers

  Browser (three views, walkthrough)
    -> src/proxy.ts            sandbox cookie; ?table=<code> joins a sandbox
    -> src/app routes          pages and the JSON API under src/app/api
    -> src/lib/service         all rules; each API route reads its body with a zod schema
                               (src/lib/http/schemas.ts), calls one service function and
                               returns JSON or {"error": string} (src/lib/http/respond.ts)
    -> src/lib/blind           redaction rules, "At a glance", allow-list projection
    -> src/lib/guard           no-judgement guard, protected attributes, draft checks
    -> src/lib/ai              scripted designer, Gemini and Claude adapters, provider
                               chain with breaker and budget, guarded wrapper
    -> src/lib/store           in-memory store, per-visitor sandbox registry
    -> src/lib/seed            the synthetic Espoo Talent Day and the story stages
    Also: src/lib/domain (types, decision rules, scoring, places, guardrail sentences),
    src/lib/matching (Second look), src/lib/geo (Espoo outline, OpenStreetMap ODbL),
    src/lib/qr (QR codes for table mode), src/lib/http (limits, schemas, JPEG check,
    rate guard, job-ad fetch). Rule: code under src/lib never imports src/components.

7.3 The data model (src/lib/domain/types.ts)

  Identity (name, contact) lives only on the Talent record. A Submission holds the
  original. Reviewers receive BlindProof, a type with no identity fields, built by
  toBlindProof in src/lib/blind/project.ts. toBlindProof copies named fields only,
  including nested ones, so a new field on a stored proof cannot leak by accident
  (test: "keeps out every field it does not name" in src/lib/blind/project.test.ts).

7.4 One proof, end to end

  1. Draft: POST /api/challenges/assist (chat) or /api/challenges/draft or
     /api/challenges/from-ad. Each wish becomes a core skill, is set aside with a reason,
     or is read as a fact about the job (src/lib/guard/job-facts.ts).
  2. Publish: POST /api/challenges. The server checks the draft again
     (src/lib/guard/check-draft.ts), publishes the job facts in fixed forms as
     "requirements", and rebuilds the need talent sees, so the company's own words and
     set-aside wishes are never published (Eval 4: "rebuilt need only").
  3. Follow-ups: POST /api/followups runs the answer through the blind layer, then asks
     the designer for two questions; they must pass the guard (checkQuestions in
     src/lib/ai/guarded.ts).
  4. Preview: POST /api/preview runs the same blind layer over the unsent text and
     stores nothing.
  5. Submit: POST /api/submissions refuses work for a task with no places left (409),
     stores the original and builds the proof: blindWork (src/lib/blind/blind-work.ts)
     redacts the answer, each question and answer, and a picture description; atAGlance
     (src/lib/blind/glance.ts) counts points, words and code and never reads meaning;
     two audit entries record that details were hidden and the shape was counted.
  6. Review: GET /api/companies/[id]/queue returns the company's own blind proofs and its
     Second look proofs, each by arrival time. The count and categories of hidden details
     are dropped; every reviewer reads the same fixed line, REVIEWER_HIDDEN_LINE in
     src/lib/domain/guardrails.ts. A reviewer can report a picture; it is removed for
     every reader (POST /api/proofs/[proofId]/picture/report).
  7. Decide: POST /api/decisions needs a registered reviewer name, feedback, and one
     whole number 1 to 5 per core skill. The server stores the total out of 10. "hold" =
     score only; POST /api/decisions/[id]/decide turns a hold into Meet or Not now.
     Adding a reviewer is one explicit, logged step: POST /api/companies/[id]/reviewers.
  8. Meet: only the author, with their private key, can accept
     (POST /api/meetings/[id]/accept) or decline. After accept, that company sees name
     and contact, never the talent id. Both sides confirm (.../confirm); status becomes
     "met" only when both have. Follow-up and hire: .../follow-up, .../hired.
  9. City: GET /api/city counts proofs, answers, answers owed per employer, meetings,
     tasks and skills, arrivals per day and the safeguards. GET /api/city/actions returns
     what a reminder needs (nothing is sent). GET /api/city/meetings.csv exports slots
     and labels, never names (src/lib/service/city-actions.ts).

7.5 The blind layer (src/lib/blind)

  Rules, not a model. redact (src/lib/blind/redact.ts) runs in order: contacts; the
  author's own name in many spellings; event company names; schools, places and
  employers (background.ts, lexicon.ts); personal details such as age, residence status,
  origin, belief, health and family (personal.ts). Every match becomes one token,
  "[hidden]", so context does not reveal what was there. Technical text is left intact
  (Eval 2: 5 of 5 answers unchanged; 16 of 16 technical texts unchanged in the table set).
  redos.test.ts keeps every pattern safe from slow inputs; text is cut to the answer
  limit before the rules run. Proof labels are neutral and unique (Eval 2: 279 distinct
  labels for 279 proofs).

7.6 The guard (src/lib/guard)

  findJudgement (no-judgement.ts) finds scores, stars, percent matches, ranks and
  verdicts ("Hire.", "Reject.") in English, Finnish and Swedish, on a normalised copy
  (normalise.ts), so lookalike letters, zero-width characters and spaced-out words do not
  slip through. protectedNames (protected.ts) finds protected attributes: age, gender,
  origin, native language, religion, health, "culture fit". guarded (src/lib/ai/guarded.ts)
  wraps every designer; a draft or question that fails is rejected. The server repeats
  the draft checks on publish, so an edited card cannot bring judging text back
  (Eval 4: biased, fake-skill and judging cards refused with HTTP 400; clean card 200).

7.7 The AI provider layer (src/lib/ai)

  - Chain (src/lib/ai/provider/select.ts, resolveChain): Gemini first (default model
    gemini-3.8-flash, GEMINI_API_KEY or GOOGLE_API_KEY), then Claude (default model
    claude-opus-5-5, ANTHROPIC_API_KEY), then the built-in scripted designer, which
    always answers. AI_PROVIDER=claude puts Claude first; AI_PROVIDER=scripted turns the
    live models off. A key switches a model on with no code change.
  - Prompts and the JSON schema live once in src/lib/ai/provider/model-designer.ts and
    prompts.ts; each adapter (src/lib/ai/gemini, src/lib/ai/claude) only sends the
    request. The prompt tells the model that people score later and forbids points,
    scales, pass marks, praise or judgement.
  - postJson (src/lib/ai/provider/http.ts): 28 seconds per attempt, one retry on 429 or
    5xx honouring retry-after (capped at 5 s). Errors carry the service, the status and
    a short reason, never the key or the text.
  - A guard-rejected reply is asked for once more, since a model rarely repeats the same
    wording; a failed service is not retried there (src/lib/ai/guarded.ts).
  - A circuit breaker pauses a provider for 5 minutes after an auth, quota, timeout or
    service failure (src/lib/ai/provider/breaker.ts). A shared budget caps live calls at
    60 per minute (src/lib/ai/provider/budget.ts). Past it, the scripted designer answers.
  - The scripted designer (src/lib/ai/scripted) has its own task for 50 professions; any
    other gets the nearest one or a general planning task. It runs without any model, so
    a draft always comes back and an event never depends on one external service.
  - Text reaches a model only after the blind rules (Eval 4: 0 of 96 checks found a
    planted identity string in 12 model requests, for drafts, practice tasks and
    follow-ups, Claude and Gemini adapters).
  - GET /api/ai/status reports which designer is active and whether it is paused, never
    the key. Each draft records which designer made it ("Drafted by ...").
  - `npm run check:ai` makes four live designer calls per provider with a key and prints
    the result; it skips providers without a key.
  - Adapter unit tests (src/lib/ai/provider/adapters.test.ts) run against mocked HTTP:
    malformed JSON, wrong shape, a draft that scores a person, a protected attribute,
    401 without retry, 429 with one retry, a timeout, and an injection inside the answer
    tags.

7.8 The sandbox model (src/lib/store)

  - src/proxy.ts gives each visitor an httpOnly cookie workproof_sandbox on the first
    page visit. API requests never create one, so cookie-less loops cannot create
    sandboxes.
  - getStore() resolves per call: the caller's sandbox with the cookie; without it, the
    shared default store, read-only (a change answers 401 "Open the site first.").
  - Up to 2000 sandboxes in memory, least recently used dropped, never one used in the
    last 30 minutes; each replaced after 12 hours; 20 new sandboxes per address per 10
    minutes (src/lib/store/sandbox.ts, creation-limit.ts). Each new sandbox is a fresh
    copy of the demo event at stage "invited".
  - A 6-character share code (no 0, O, 1 or I) lets a phone and a booth screen share one
    event: ?table=<code> goes through /api/demo/join.
  - So every judge gets a private, resettable copy of the whole event, and nothing one
    visitor does changes another visitor's demo.

7.9 Limits and hardening (src/lib/http)

  Text caps per field (limits.ts: answer 8000, need 1200, chat message 5000, feedback
  1000 characters, and more); request body 128 kB; picture 300 kB; 60 changing requests
  per address per minute; 30 story/reset rebuilds per minute counted apart. The job-ad
  fetch (ad-fetch.ts) treats a visitor's link as untrusted: public https only, no
  private, loopback, link-local, carrier-grade NAT or unique-local addresses, redirects
  only on the same host and each target re-checked, at most 300 kB within 5 seconds.
  Console output is restricted to a few named warnings; the end-to-end run fails on any
  browser console error.

8. DESIGN DECISIONS AND TRADE-OFFS
====================================================================

D1  Humans score; software only adds up.
    The brief forbids automated decisions, and the city asked for visible scores. Both
    are met by one rule: only a named reviewer gives points, on a fixed 1 to 5 scale
    with words per level ("Not shown" to "Shown well, with reasons", SCORE_LEVELS in
    src/lib/domain/scoring.ts). Software computes the total and sorts a task's scored
    work by it. It adds no judgement of its own. Trade-off accepted: reading takes a
    person's time, so the places cap (D4) keeps it bounded.

D2  Redaction by rules, not by a model.
    Rules are deterministic, testable with planted strings, cheap, and work offline.
    The person sees the result before sending (/api/preview) and can remove anything
    else. A model would add cost, latency and another place where personal data goes.

D3  One neutral token and no count.
    Every hidden detail becomes "[hidden]", and a reviewer never sees how many were
    hidden or of which kind, because a count would hint at a background (for example, a
    long list of foreign place names). The author sees the count; the reviewer sees one
    fixed sentence (Eval 4: "hidden count shown to the author only").

D4  A fixed number of places per task (5 to 30, default 12).
    Answers what the brief calls overwhelm: an employer can read every answer, and a
    professional knows the work will be read. A sixth answer to a 5-place task gets 409
    and no waiting list.

D5  Arrival order, and no ranking of people.
    Work waiting to be read is in arrival order. The only other order is a task's top
    work, by the reviewers' own totals. No match percentage, no recommendation of a
    person. Second look compares tasks by shared skills, never people.

D6  An allow-list projection.
    BlindProof is built by naming each field to copy. Deleting fields would leak any
    field added later; an allow-list cannot.

D7  The name follows consent.
    Identity reaches a company only after the person accepts. A decline keeps it hidden
    for good (Eval 4: status after decline stays declined even after a later accept; name
    in responses: false). The city never sees names.

D8  The AI drafts; it never touches people.
    The AI's work is upstream of people: turning a company's need into a fair task, and
    asking about the person's own choices. This removes the biggest risk class under the
    EU AI Act (evaluating, filtering or ranking candidates) by design, while still using
    AI where companies need help most: describing the need (P2).

D9  A scripted designer under every model.
    A public event cannot depend on one AI vendor's uptime or quota. The chain falls
    through Gemini, Claude and a built-in designer, with a breaker and a budget, so a
    draft always comes back.

D10 No login, private sandboxes.
    Logins are friction and a crash point at event start (P7). Each visitor gets a
    private copy of the event behind an httpOnly cookie; the shared copy is read-only;
    the private key never appears in a URL, so it does not leak through logs, history or
    referrers.

D11 The company's own words are not published.
    The server rebuilds the need that talent sees from the approved fields, so a biased
    sentence a company typed never reaches talent, even if it was edited back in.

D12 Job facts in fixed forms.
    A work language, a language level or a licence the law asks for is legitimate
    information. It is published in fixed forms under "Good to know", separate from the
    scored skills, so it informs without filtering by background.

D13 One typed source for every claim.
    src/app/for-judges/facts.ts and evidence.ts feed /for-judges, /llms.txt,
    /llms-full.txt, /submission.json, /brief and the README blocks. facts.test.ts fails
    if a named route or path does not exist; submission.json/build.test.ts checks its
    numbers equal evidence.ts; `npm run evidence` writes nothing when a check fails.

9. RESPONSIBLE AI AND DATA PROTECTION
====================================================================

9.1 What is automated and what is not (docs/responsible-ai.md)

  Draft a task from a company's sentence ......... AI (or the scripted designer)
  Approve and publish a company task ............. a named person at the company
  Two follow-up questions about own choices ...... AI, on redacted text
  Remove known personal details .................. fixed rules in code, not AI
  Describe the shape of the work ................. fixed code (points, words, code)
  Order the work waiting ......................... arrival time only
  Mark where each skill shows up ................. fixed code with cue words, no number
  Score each core skill 1 to 5 ................... a registered, named reviewer
  Add up points, order a task's top work ......... fixed arithmetic on the human points
  Decide Meet or Not now ......................... the same named reviewer
  Share a proof with a second employer ........... only with the person's consent
  Reveal the name ................................ only after the person accepts

  No AI output is ever about a person.

9.2 EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), design position

  - Annex III point 4 lists AI for recruitment or selection, including systems that
    analyse and filter applications and evaluate candidates, as high-risk.
  - WorkProof's AI does not evaluate, filter, rank or profile candidates. It drafts a
    task from a company's need and asks two questions about the person's answer. People
    read the work, score it and decide.
  - Article 6(3) describes Annex III systems that are not considered high-risk, such as
    those performing a narrow procedural or preparatory task; a system that profiles
    natural persons is always high-risk. WorkProof is designed to stay inside that narrow
    role, and the guard and the evals exist to keep it there. The provider documents
    this assessment under Article 6(4); the repository states it as a design position.
  - The high-risk duties are built anyway: human oversight (Article 14), logs of what the
    system did (Article 12), and telling people when AI was involved (drafts carry
    "Drafted by ...").
  - No analysis of voices or faces. Emotion recognition in the workplace is prohibited
    under Article 5.

9.3 GDPR

  - Article 22: no decision based solely on automated processing. Every Meet or Not now
    is recorded with a registered reviewer's name and a written reason; the city counts
    decisions without a registered name: 0 (Eval 3 (e)).
  - Article 5(1)(c) data minimisation and Article 25 data protection by design: see R3
    in section 5.2.
  - A pilot run by the city adds a data protection impact assessment (Article 35), a
    privacy notice and a lawful basis (docs/responsible-ai.md, "What a pilot would add").

9.4 Logging (appendAudit in src/lib/service/support.ts)

  The audit log records AI drafts and follow-up questions, details hidden per proof, the
  "At a glance" count, consent recorded or withdrawn, each human decision, invitations,
  acceptance and confirmations. Each entry names an actor (ai, human or system) and never
  contains the person's identity. Visible at /city/log.

9.5 Consent

  Second look is off until the person turns it on and can be withdrawn at any time:
  employers who have not answered lose access; an employer that answered keeps its
  decision and its own feedback, not the proof text (Eval 4: "work shown to the Second
  look employer after withdrawal: false; its decision kept: true"). A picture is sent
  only after the person confirms it shows only the work (Eval 4: HTTP 400 without the
  check). Only the person, with their key, can accept an invitation (Eval 4: HTTP 400 with
  no key, 403 with a wrong key, 403 for a guessed confirm, 403 for another company).

13. THE PATH FROM PROTOTYPE TO PILOT
====================================================================

The prototype runs end to end today on synthetic data. The README "Roadmap to a pilot"
names the steps to a real Espoo event:

  1. Sign-in for companies and the city, with ownership checks on every route.
  2. A database in place of the in-memory store.
  3. ESCO skill ids on task skills, so tasks share the EU skills vocabulary.
  4. A pilot event with 5 employers, one task each, measuring answers given and meetings
     confirmed.

Because the service rules sit in src/lib behind thin routes, with the store resolved per
call (getStore), these steps replace the store and add an auth layer without changing
the rules that the evals test. The meeting list already exports as CSV for the city's
existing event tools, and the docs list the pilot's compliance steps (DPIA, privacy
notice, lawful basis, retention and deletion, WCAG 2.1 AA audit).

Who benefits:
  - Companies: a short task instead of a CV pile, a bounded number of answers, and
    people who already showed the skill.
  - Professionals, including those new to Finland: judged on the work, not the CV, the
    school or the accent; feedback with a reason; their name shared only with a yes.
  - The City of Espoo: a free service with measurable outcomes per event and company,
    built to the EU AI Act and GDPR from the start.
